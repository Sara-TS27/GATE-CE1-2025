\subsection*{Problem}
At how many points will the curves $y=x^{2}$ and $y=-x^{2}-2x-1$ intersect in the real $(x, y)$ plane?
\begin{enumerate}
    \item[(A)] 0
    \item[(B)] 1
    \item[(C)] 2
    \item[(D)] 3
\end{enumerate}

\subsection*{Solution (Direct Method)}
Equating the two curves to find points of intersection:
\[
x^{2} = -x^{2} - 2x - 1
\]
\[
2x^{2} + 2x + 1 = 0
\]

Using the quadratic formula $x = \frac{-b \pm \sqrt{b^{2} - 4ac}}{2a}$:
\[
\Delta = b^{2} - 4ac = (2)^{2} - 4(2)(1) = 4 - 8 = -4
\]

Since the discriminant $\Delta < 0$, there are no real solutions for $x$, meaning the two parabolas do not intersect in the real $(x, y)$ plane.

\textbf{Answer:} \textbf{(A) 0}

\subsection*{Solution (Pencil of Conics / Matrix Formulation)}
Representing the curves in general conic form $x^{\top}Vx + 2u^{\top}x + f = 0$ where $x = \begin{pmatrix} x \\ y \end{pmatrix}$:

\begin{itemize}
    \item Curve 1: $x^2 - y = 0 \implies V_1 = \begin{pmatrix}1 & 0\\0 & 0\end{pmatrix}, \quad u_1 = \begin{pmatrix}0\\-0.5\end{pmatrix}, \quad f_1 = 0$
    \item Curve 2: $x^2 + 2x + y + 1 = 0 \implies V_2 = \begin{pmatrix}1 & 0\\0 & 0\end{pmatrix}, \quad u_2 = \begin{pmatrix}1\\0.5\end{pmatrix}, \quad f_2 = 1$
\end{itemize}

Forming the radical line equation by setting $\mu = -1$:
\[
2(u_1 - u_2)^{\top}x + (f_1 - f_2) = 0
\]
\[
2\begin{pmatrix}-1 & -1\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}x\\y\end{pmatrix} - 1 = 0 \implies 2x + 2y + 1 = 0
\]

Parametrizing the radical line with base point $h = \begin{pmatrix}0\\-0.5\end{pmatrix}$ and direction $m = \begin{pmatrix}1\\-1\end{pmatrix}$:
\[
\kappa = \frac{-m^{\top}(V_1 h + u_1) \pm \sqrt{[m^{\top}(V_1 h + u_1)]^2 - g(h)(m^{\top}V_1 m)}}{m^{\top}V_1 m}
\]
\[
\kappa = \frac{-0.5 \pm \sqrt{0.25 - 0.5}}{1} = -0.5 \pm 0.5i
\]

Since $\kappa$ is complex, no real intersection points exist.
